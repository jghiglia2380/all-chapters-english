📚 Day 1 Recall: Contract Evaluation Essentials

Activity 2: Consumer Rights Application

⏱️ Time: 12 minutes (3 minutes per scenario)

Scenario 1: Bait-and-Switch Tactics

Car dealership advertised "$199/month lease—no money down!" When you arrive, the contract shows: $2,500 due at signing, $249/month, mandatory $1,200 maintenance package, $395 acquisition fee.

Applicable Laws:

  • FTC Act - prohibits deceptive advertising
  • Truth in Lending Act (TILA) - requires clear credit disclosure
  • State consumer protection laws - vary by state

Scenario 2: Defective Product with "No Warranty" Clause

You purchased a used appliance "as-is, no warranty" for $400. It failed within one week. Seller refuses refund citing "as-is" clause in sales receipt.

Legal Principles:

  • Implied warranties (merchantability, fitness) may apply even with "as-is" if seller is dealer
  • Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act governs consumer product warranties
  • Fraud/misrepresentation may void "as-is" terms if seller knew of defect

Scenario 3: Forced Arbitration for Serious Harm

You signed an apartment lease with mandatory arbitration clause waiving right to jury trial. You suffered serious injury from landlord's negligence (failed to repair known hazard). Arbitration clause says disputes must be resolved through private arbitration.

Scenario 4: Cooling-Off Period Rights

You signed an expensive home improvement contract ($8,000) during a high-pressure door-to-door sales pitch. Next day, you realized the price is 3x market rate. Contract says "all sales final."

Federal Cooling-Off Rule:

Door-to-door sales over $25 have a 3-day cooling-off period. Seller must inform you of cancellation rights.

Activity 3: Red Flag Identification Challenge

⏱️ Time: 10 minutes (3 rounds)

Round 1: Liability and Warranty Disclaimers (3 min)

Classify each clause as Acceptable, Concerning, or Unacceptable:

Clause 1: "Product sold as-is with no warranty of any kind" [Used car, $15,000]

Clause 2: "Company not liable for any damages including those caused by our negligence"

Clause 3: "Limited warranty covers defects for 90 days; excludes consequential damages"

Clause 4: "Customer waives all claims against Company for any reason whatsoever"

Round 2: Termination and Modification (3 min)

Evaluate these contract provisions:

Good: "Either party may terminate with 30 days notice"

Mutual termination rights = Fair

Bad: "Company may terminate anytime; Customer needs 90 days notice + $500 fee"

One-sided = Unfair

Concerning: "Company may change terms by posting on website; continued use = acceptance"

Acceptable: "Annual contract with month-to-month rollover after initial term"

Provides flexibility after commitment

Round 3: Financial and Payment Terms (4 min)

Calculate hidden costs and identify problems:

Problem 1: Service advertised as "$9.99/month"

Contract shows: $9.99 + $2.99 processing + $4.99 convenience + taxes

Problem 2: "$500 deposit, fully refundable"

Fine print lists 15 conditions that forfeit deposit (normal wear and tear, cleaning not meeting standard, late notice, etc.)

Problem 3: "Low monthly payment of $50"

For 84 months = $4,200 total for $1,800 item

Activity 4: Negotiation Strategy Workshop

⏱️ Time: 12 minutes

Negotiation Scripts Practice

Weak Approach:

"Can we make the termination fee lower?"

Strong Approach:

"I'm very interested in signing, but the $500 early termination fee for a $40/month membership is excessive—that's 12.5 months of dues. Industry standard is 2-3 months maximum. Could we reduce it to $120 (3 months)? If so, I'm ready to sign today."

Why the strong approach works:

  • Shows genuine interest (not just complaining)
  • Provides objective benchmark (industry standard)
  • Offers specific alternative (not vague "lower")
  • Creates urgency (ready to sign today)

Practice Scenarios - Write Strong Negotiation Scripts:

Scenario 1: $500 gym termination fee (annual dues $480)

Scenario 2: Employment 2-year non-compete covering entire industry in 5-state region

Scenario 3: Automatic price increase clause: "Price may increase up to 15% annually"

Negotiation Success Factors:

DO:
  • ✓ Research standard terms beforehand
  • ✓ Be specific about what you want changed
  • ✓ Explain why it's reasonable
  • ✓ Show willingness to sign if terms improve
  • ✓ Remain professional and respectful
DON'T:
  • ✗ Make vague requests ("better terms")
  • ✗ Be confrontational or demanding
  • ✗ Negotiate every single clause
  • ✗ Accept "this is non-negotiable" without trying
  • ✗ Sign if critical terms remain unacceptable

🎯 Key Takeaways

✓ Systematic analysis works - Using structured evaluation ensures you don't miss critical details

✓ Many terms are negotiable - Despite being presented as "standard," you can often get better terms by asking

✓ Consumer laws protect you - Rights that cannot be contracted away (unconscionability, cooling-off periods, warranty protections)

✓ Red flags demand scrutiny - As-is, non-refundable, binding arbitration, automatic renewal, broad liability waivers

✓ Walking away is powerful - Your best leverage is willingness to decline bad deals

✓ Research beats guessing - Know what's standard vs. excessive in your market

✓ Document everything - Get promises in writing; contracts override verbal assurances

✓ Never sign under pressure - If deal won't wait for careful review, it's probably not a good deal
