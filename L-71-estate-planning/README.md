# L-71: Introduction to Estate Planning

## Chapter Overview
This chapter provides a foundational introduction to estate planning for high school students, covering essential legal instruments that protect individuals and families during incapacity and after death. The content emphasizes that estate planning is not exclusively for the wealthy or elderly—it's about protection, control, and care for loved ones at any life stage.

## Learning Objectives
- Define essential estate planning documents: wills, trusts, power of attorney, and healthcare directives
- Explain the probate process and strategies for avoiding or minimizing it
- Compare revocable and irrevocable trusts and understand when each is appropriate
- Analyze how beneficiary designations interact with wills and why they're critical
- Evaluate guardianship designations and their importance for families with minor children
- Make informed decisions about basic estate planning needs appropriate for current life stage

## Target Audience
- **Age Level:** 17-18 years old (high school seniors)
- **Prior Knowledge:** Basic understanding of legal documents, family structures, financial accounts
- **Scope:** Introductory—focuses on fundamental protection instruments, not complex tax strategies

## Key Topics Covered

### Day 1: Foundation and Core Concepts
1. **Wills**: Types, purposes, what happens without one (intestate succession)
2. **Trusts**: Revocable vs. irrevocable, living trusts, when they're appropriate
3. **Beneficiary Designations**: How they override wills, common mistakes
4. **Probate**: What it is, costs, timeline, how to avoid it
5. **Powers of Attorney**: Financial and healthcare, protection during incapacity
6. **Healthcare Directives**: Living wills, advance directives, end-of-life planning
7. **Guardianship**: Designating who raises minor children

### Day 2: Practical Application (Learning Lab)
- **Activity 1**: Will vs. No Will Simulation (15 min)
- **Activity 2**: Trust Comparison Tool (15 min)
- **Activity 3**: Power of Attorney & Healthcare Directive Builder (10 min)
- **Activity 4**: Beneficiary Designation Audit (15 min)

## Common Student Misconceptions Addressed

1. **"Estate planning is only for wealthy/elderly people"**
   - *Correction*: Focuses on young adult scenarios; healthcare directives needed at 18

2. **"My family will figure it out"**
   - *Correction*: Shows court battles, family conflict, unintended outcomes without planning

3. **"A will avoids probate"**
   - *Correction*: Explains wills go THROUGH probate; trusts/beneficiaries avoid it

4. **"Beneficiaries don't matter if I have a will"**
   - *Correction*: Demonstrates that beneficiaries TRUMP wills

5. **"I can just tell people my wishes"**
   - *Correction*: Shows legal documents required for enforceability

## Real-World Connections

### Scenarios Used
1. **Maya's Healthcare Crisis**: 27-year-old without healthcare directive; family conflict
2. **Chen Family Success**: Young parents with comprehensive planning; smooth transition
3. **Marcus's Intestacy Disaster**: 29-year-old dies without will; partner gets nothing
4. **Various Activity Scenarios**: Single professionals, blended families, divorced parents, recent graduates

### Life Stage Relevance
- **Age 18**: Healthcare directive and financial POA become immediately relevant
- **Serious Relationship**: Will becomes important (unmarried partners have no automatic rights)
- **Marriage**: Beneficiary updates critical
- **Children**: Guardian designation non-negotiable; trust consideration essential
- **Divorce**: Immediate updates to all documents
