The Challenge

Maya, 27, was critically injured in an accident. Her fiancé Jordan knew her wishes, but without a healthcare directive, Maya's estranged parents made all medical decisions—decisions contradicting what Maya would have wanted. Meanwhile, no one could access Maya's bank accounts to pay her rent because she hadn't designated a financial power of attorney.

How can understanding estate planning tools protect you during incapacity and ensure your wishes are honored after death—even as a young adult?

Learning Objectives

  • Define essential estate planning documents and their purposes
  • Explain the probate process and costs of dying without proper planning
  • Understand how beneficiary designations override wills
  • Recognize when trusts are necessary versus when simple wills suffice
  • Create personal action plan for obtaining incapacity protection documents
Core Concepts
TermDefinition
Will Legal document directing asset distribution after death and naming guardians for minor children. Only effective upon death.
Intestate Dying without a will. State law determines who inherits using rigid formulas that rarely match personal preferences.
Probate Court-supervised process of validating will, paying debts, distributing assets. Public, costly (3-7% of estate), time-consuming (6-24 months).
Trust Legal arrangement where trustee holds assets for beneficiaries. Avoids probate, controls distribution timing, provides privacy.
Beneficiary Designation Form naming who receives specific accounts directly (retirement, life insurance). TRUMPS wills—designations override any will provision.
Power of Attorney Financial POA: authorizes someone to handle money if incapacitated. Healthcare POA: authorizes medical decisions. Durable = remains effective during incapacity.
Healthcare Directive Document specifying medical treatment preferences and naming healthcare proxy. Also called Living Will or Advance Directive.
Guardian Person designated in will to raise minor children if parents die or become incapacitated. Court decides if not designated.
Background: Estate planning isn't only for the wealthy or elderly—it's about protection and control. Incapacity from accidents or illness can happen at any age. Your Beneficiary Review Checklist
List all accounts with beneficiary designations (401k, IRA, life insurance, POD accounts)
Document current primary and contingent beneficiaries for each
Check for red flags: ex-spouse listed, minor children direct, "my estate", no contingent
Update beneficiaries after: marriage, divorce, births, deaths
Set annual review reminder (suggest January 15 every year)
