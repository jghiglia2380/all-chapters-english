Healthcare Directive Planning

Financial Power of Attorney Planning

A financial POA authorizes someone to manage your finances if you're incapacitated—preventing costly conservatorship.

Financial Agent Selection

Who is trustworthy, responsible, and financially capable?

Selection Criteria:
  • ✓ Financially responsible and trustworthy
  • ✓ Understands financial matters (banking, investments, taxes)
  • ✓ Detail-oriented and organized
  • ✓ Available to act quickly if needed
  • ✓ No conflicts of interest

Scope of Authority

Durable (Immediate Effect)
Effective immediately; remains effective during incapacity. Recommended by most attorneys.
Springing (Upon Incapacity)
Only effective when doctor certifies incapacity. Can delay access when needed most.
Banking (access accounts, pay bills, transfer funds)
Investments (manage accounts, buy/sell securities)
Real Estate (pay mortgage, maintain property)
Taxes (file returns, communicate with IRS)
Legal Affairs (hire attorneys, manage legal matters)
Government Benefits (apply for benefits, manage insurance)

Scenario Comparison: With vs. Without Documents

See the real-world impact of having or not having incapacity protection documents.

Scenario: 28-Year-Old in Coma After Car Accident

Jamie, critically injured, unconscious for 3 weeks, uncertain recovery.

✓ WITH DOCUMENTS

Healthcare:
Named healthcare proxy (partner Alex) makes decisions immediately in consultation with doctors
Financial:
Named financial agent (Jamie's sister) pays rent, car payment, insurance from Jamie's accounts
Timeline:
Immediate authorization; bills paid on time
Cost:
$0 (documents already in place)
Family Impact:
Family focuses on Jamie's recovery, not legal battles; wishes honored

✗ WITHOUT DOCUMENTS

Healthcare:
Parents vs. partner argue over treatment; hospital delays while determining who can consent
Financial:
No one can access Jamie's accounts; family must petition court for conservatorship
Timeline:
2-3 months to get conservatorship; bills go unpaid, eviction notice received
Cost:
$5,000-8,000 in legal fees + credit damage
Family Impact:
Family torn apart by legal conflicts; partner excluded despite relationship; financial disaster
Net Benefit: $4,600-7,600 savings + immeasurable family harmony

Scenario: 34-Year-Old Stroke Victim, Unmarried with Partner

Taylor, severe stroke, partially paralyzed, long recovery. Partner of 6 years (Morgan), not married.

✓ WITH DOCUMENTS

Morgan has full legal authority despite not being married
Morgan coordinates rehabilitation, makes decisions, manages finances
Quick decisions support Taylor's recovery without legal obstacles
Cost: $300 (documents prepared 2 years ago)

✗ WITHOUT DOCUMENTS

Parents make all medical decisions; Morgan excluded from discussions
Morgan is legal stranger despite 6-year relationship
Can't get medical information; forbidden from visiting by parents
Shared home at risk (can't access Taylor's portion of mortgage)
Cost: $7,000+ conservatorship + relationship destroyed
KEY LESSON: For unmarried couples (including same-sex couples), these documents are CRITICAL—you have NO automatic legal standing without them

Key Takeaways

  • • At age 18, parents have NO automatic authority—you need documents regardless of family relationships
  • • Unmarried couples have NO legal standing without these documents—absolutely critical
  • • Cost of documents ($200-400) is trivial compared to conservatorship ($5,000-8,000) and family conflict
