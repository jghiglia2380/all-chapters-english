Beneficiary Designation Audit

Beneficiary designations TRUMP wills—ensure yours are correct

Critical Principle

Beneficiary designations override your will—they control distribution regardless of what your will says.

For most people, largest assets (retirement accounts, life insurance) pass by beneficiary designation, not will. A perfect will is worthless if your beneficiaries are wrong.

Practice: Proper Beneficiary Planning

For each life situation, determine appropriate beneficiary designations:

Situation A: Single, Age 25, $15K in 401(k), Dating but Not Engaged

Recommended: Primary = Parents (or siblings); Contingent = Other family. DON'T name boyfriend/girlfriend unless engaged.

Situation B: Married, Ages 30/32, Two Young Children, $200K Retirement, $250K Life Insurance Each

Recommended: Retirement Primary = Spouse (tax benefits), Contingent = TRUST for children (NOT children directly). Life Insurance Primary = TRUST for children.

Situation C: Recently Divorced Parent, Age 37, $180K Retirement, $500K Life Insurance, 10-Year-Old Child

URGENT ACTION NEEDED: All beneficiaries currently list ex-spouse!
Recommended: Primary = TRUST for child; Contingent = Child's guardian or grandparents. This is #1 priority after divorce!

Step 4: Update Plan

Create trust if needed (for minor children)
Complete and submit updated forms
Confirm receipt and filing
Keep copies with estate planning documents

Step 5: Set Annual Review Reminder

Set a calendar event for January 15 every year: "Annual beneficiary designation review"

Also review immediately after: Marriage, divorce, births, deaths, new job/accounts

Beneficiary Best Practices

✓ DO

  • • Review beneficiaries annually
  • • Always name contingent beneficiaries
  • • Update within 30 days of life events
  • • Name individuals or trusts directly
  • • Use trusts for minor children
  • • Name spouse for retirement accounts (tax benefits)
  • • Coordinate with overall estate plan

✗ DON'T

  • • Name minor children directly
  • • Name "my estate" as beneficiary
  • • Forget to update after divorce
  • • Leave contingent beneficiary blank
  • • Assume your will controls these accounts
  • • Go more than 3 years without review
  • • Name deceased people
