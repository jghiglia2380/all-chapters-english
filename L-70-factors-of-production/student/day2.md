## 📚 Day 1 Recall: The Four Factors

Every economy uses four factors of production:
- **Land** (natural resources → rent)
- **Labor** (human effort → wages)
- **Capital** (tools/equipment → interest)
- **Entrepreneurship** (risk/innovation → profit)

Today, you'll apply these concepts to real businesses and your own life.

### ⚠️ Common Mistakes:
- **Robot as Labor:** Robots are capital (human-made tools), not labor. Labor requires human effort.
- **Manager expertise as Entrepreneurship:** Management skills are labor (human capital). Entrepreneurship is specifically about risk-taking and organizing a business.
- **Starting a business as Capital:** The act of starting a business is entrepreneurship. Capital refers to the physical equipment purchased.

### Business Scenarios:

#### ☕ Coffee Shop
**Description:** A cozy coffee shop in downtown {{STATE_NAME}} serving espresso drinks, pastries, and light breakfast.

**Cost Hints:**
- **Land (Rent):** Retail space lease: ~$2,000-4,000/month
- **Labor (Wages):** 1 manager + 3 baristas (~8 hours/day). Consider {{STATE_MIN_WAGE}} minimum wage.
- **Capital (Interest):** Espresso machine ($15,000), grinder ($2,000), POS system ($1,500), furniture ($5,000). Loan at 7% interest ≈ $140/month
- **Other Costs:** Coffee beans, milk, pastries, utilities (~$4,000/month)
- **Revenue:** Average ticket $8, 150 customers/day, 26 days/month = $31,200

**Typical Breakdown:**
- Rent: $3,000
- Wages: $12,000
- Interest: $140
- Other: $4,000
- **Total Costs:** $19,140
- **Revenue:** $31,200
- **Profit:** $12,060 (38.7% margin) ✅ Strong!

---

#### 💻 Tech Startup
**Description:** A small tech company building a mobile app for local service providers.

**Cost Hints:**
- **Land (Rent):** Co-working space: ~$500-2,000/month
- **Labor (Wages):** 2 developers ($70k each), 1 designer ($50k) = $15,833/month
- **Capital (Interest):** Computers ($10,000), minimal loan needed ≈ $50/month
- **Other Costs:** Software licenses, servers, marketing (~$2,700/month)
- **Revenue:** SaaS model: 500 subscribers × $30/month = $15,000

**Typical Breakdown:**
- Rent: $1,200
- Wages: $15,833
- Interest: $50
- Other: $2,700
- **Total Costs:** $19,783
- **Revenue:** $15,000
- **Profit:** -$4,783 (Loss) ⚠️ Common for startups in growth phase

---

#### 🌾 Family Farm
**Description:** A 200-acre farm growing crops and selling at farmers markets.

**Cost Hints:**
- **Land (Rent):** Property tax ~$2,000/year ÷ 12 = $167/month (if owned)
- **Labor (Wages):** Family labor + seasonal workers ≈ $6,000/month
- **Capital (Interest):** Tractor, equipment ($100,000) at 6% = $500/month
- **Other Costs:** Seeds, fertilizer, fuel, insurance (~$3,000/month)
- **Revenue:** 200 acres × $400/acre ÷ 12 months = $6,667

**Note:** Farms often have seasonal income concentrated in harvest months.

---

#### 🏗️ Construction Company
**Description:** A residential construction company building custom homes.

**Cost Hints:**
- **Land (Rent):** Office/yard space: ~$3,000/month
- **Labor (Wages):** Project manager + 5 skilled workers + admin ≈ $22,917/month
- **Capital (Interest):** Trucks, tools, equipment ($140,000) at 7% = $817/month
- **Other Costs:** Materials, insurance, permits (~$20,000/month)
- **Revenue:** 2.5 homes/year × $50k profit ÷ 12 months ≈ $50,000

---

### Reflection Questions:
1. **Which factor is most expensive for this business?**
   - Coffee: Labor (35-40% of costs)
   - Tech: Labor (80%+ of costs)
   - Farm: Capital and inputs
   - Construction: Labor, then materials

2. **How could the entrepreneur increase profit?**
   - Increase revenue (raise prices, increase volume, add services)
   - Reduce labor costs (automation, efficiency, better training)
   - Reduce rent (relocate, negotiate lease)
   - Reduce capital costs (buy used equipment, lease instead)
   - Improve efficiency (reduce waste, better inventory management)

## Activity 3: Your Personal Factor Contribution
**Duration:** 15 minutes

### 📋 Instructions:
Calculate how YOU contribute to the economy through labor and capital.

### Part A: Your Labor Contribution

**Example Calculation:**
- Job: Retail sales associate
- Hourly wage: $15
- Hours per week: 20
- **Weekly income:** $15 × 20 = $300
- **Annual income:** $300 × 52 = $15,600

### Part B: Your Human Capital

Check all that apply and add the percentages:
- [ ] High school diploma (0% boost - baseline)
- [ ] Some college (+15% boost)
- [ ] Associate's degree (+30% boost)
- [ ] Bachelor's degree planned (+50% boost)
- [ ] Professional certifications (+10% boost)
- [ ] Specialized skills training (+5% boost)

**Example:** If you have some college (+15%) and plan to get a bachelor's degree (+50%), your human capital boost = 65%

**Projected income:** $15,600 × 1.65 = $25,740

### Part C: Your Capital Contribution

When you save money, you're providing capital to the economy!

**Example Calculation:**
- Current savings: $1,000
- Interest rate (APY): 4.5%
- **Annual interest earned:** $1,000 × 0.045 = $45

### 💡 Key Insights:

**If annual income < $20,000:**
Your current labor income is modest. Consider investing in education or skills training to boost your human capital and earning potential.

**If annual income $20,000-$50,000:**
Solid income foundation! Growing your human capital through certifications or advanced training could significantly boost your earnings.

**If annual income > $50,000:**
Strong earning potential! Consider diversifying income sources by building capital (savings/investments) to earn interest alongside wages.
