Factors of Production

Quick Reference Guide

🌍
LAND
💰 Rent
Definition: All natural resources including physical space, minerals, water, forests, and anything that exists in nature before humans do anything with it.
Examples:
  • Real estate locations (office buildings, retail space, farmland)
  • Natural resources (oil, natural gas, coal, minerals, metals)
  • Water (rivers, lakes, oceans, freshwater sources)
  • Forests and timber
  • Sunlight and wind (for renewable energy)
  • Fishing rights, mineral rights, airspace
Payment: RENT
  • Monthly apartment/house rent
  • Commercial lease payments
  • Oil drilling royalties
  • Mining rights fees
👷
LABOR
💰 Wages
Definition: All human effort—both physical and mental work applied to production. Includes everything from manual work to intellectual creativity.
Examples:
  • Unskilled Labor: Entry-level retail, basic food service, manual warehouse work
  • Skilled Labor: Healthcare professionals, software developers, licensed electricians, financial analysts
  • Professional Labor: Doctors, lawyers, engineers, teachers
  • Creative Labor: Artists, writers, designers, musicians
Payment: WAGES/SALARIES
  • Hourly wages (e.g., $15/hour for retail)
  • Annual salaries (e.g., $60,000/year for engineer)
  • Commission (salespeople earning % of sales)
  • Tips (service industry workers)
Human Capital:
  • Human capital = skills, knowledge, experience that make workers more productive
  • College degree can increase wages by 50-84% compared to high school alone
  • Professional certifications boost earning potential by 10-20%
🏭
CAPITAL
💰 Interest
Definition: Human-made resources used to produce other goods and services. Includes factories, equipment, tools, and machinery. NOT money!
Examples:
  • Physical Structures: Factories, warehouses, office buildings, data centers
  • Equipment & Machinery: Assembly line robots, computers, medical equipment, construction machinery
  • Transportation: Delivery trucks, cargo ships, airplanes, rail cars
  • Tools: Hand tools, power tools, specialized equipment
Payment: INTEREST
  • Businesses borrow money to buy capital equipment and pay interest on loans
  • When you save money, banks lend it to businesses—you earn interest
  • Typical business loan rates: 5-10% interest
Important: Capital ≠ Money
  • ❌ Cash, stocks, bonds are NOT capital (they're financial assets)
  • ✅ Factories, computers, trucks ARE capital (physical productive assets)
  • Money is used to BUY capital, but money itself doesn't produce anything
💡
ENTREPRENEURSHIP
💰 Profit
Definition: The ability to combine land, labor, and capital in new ways to create value. Involves risk-taking, innovation, and organizing all other factors.
The 4 Roles of an Entrepreneur:
  • 1. Risk-Taker: Invests time and money with uncertain outcomes (20% of businesses fail in year one)
  • 2. Innovator: Creates new products, services, or processes that didn't exist before
  • 3. Organizer: Coordinates land, labor, and capital efficiently to produce output
  • 4. Decision-Maker: Makes choices about what to produce, how to produce, and for whom
Payment: PROFIT (or Loss)
  • Profit = Total Revenue - (Rent + Wages + Interest + Other Costs)
  • Entrepreneurs earn profit ONLY if revenue exceeds costs
  • If costs exceed revenue, entrepreneurs take a LOSS
  • Profit is the reward for successful risk-taking; loss is the cost of failure
Success Rates:
  • 20% of businesses fail in year 1
  • 50% fail by year 5
  • 65% fail by year 10
  • Only 35% survive 10+ years
