- Bottom note: "Premiums are paid every month and do NOT count toward your deductible"

**Instructional use:** Display during Day 1 "How the Cost Structure Works Together" section. Students reference during Station 1 calculations.

### Asset 2: Plan Type Comparison Chart
**Type:** Comparison table
**Format:** 8.5×11, two-column Pros/Cons layout, color-coded
**File naming:** `L-72_Plan_Types_Comparison.png`

**Purpose:** Provide a clean, visual comparison of HMO, PPO, EPO, and HDHP with pros and cons for each.

**Design specifications:**
Four cards (one per plan type), each with:

**HMO (Blue card):**
- **Pros:** Lower monthly premiums, predictable copays, coordinated care through PCP
- **Cons:** Requires referrals to see specialists, no out-of-network coverage (except emergencies), less flexibility

**PPO (Green card):**
- **Pros:** No referrals needed, can see any doctor, some out-of-network coverage
- **Cons:** Higher monthly premiums, higher cost-sharing, more expensive than HMO

**EPO (Yellow card):**
- **Pros:** No referrals needed like PPO, lower cost than PPO, moderate premiums
- **Cons:** No out-of-network coverage at all (except emergencies), network restrictions

**HDHP (Orange card):**
- **Pros:** Lowest monthly premiums, HSA-eligible (triple tax advantage), great for healthy people
- **Cons:** High deductible ($3,000-$7,000), pay full cost until deductible met, risky for chronic conditions

**Layout:** 2×2 grid, not a dense table. Each pro/con in one line max. Visual icons for each plan type.

**Instructional use:** Day 1 plan type section. Students reference when answering reflection prompts and during Station 2 recommendations.

### Asset 3: Where Coverage Comes From — Decision Tree
**Type:** Flow diagram / decision tree
**Format:** 5×7 card (printable reference card for students)
**File naming:** `L-72_Coverage_Sources_Decision_Tree.png`

**Purpose:** Help students navigate where to find health coverage based on their employment and income situation.

**Decision tree flow:**

```
START: Do you have a job that offers health insurance?
├─ YES → Is the employee contribution affordable (<9.5% of income)?
│   ├─ YES → Take employer plan
│   └─ NO → Check marketplace for subsidized plans
└─ NO → Check your income
    ├─ Low income → Check Medicaid eligibility (varies by state)
    ├─ Moderate income (100-400% FPL) → Healthcare.gov marketplace with subsidies
    └─ Higher income (>400% FPL) → Healthcare.gov marketplace (unsubsidized)

Special case: Turning 26 or losing coverage?
→ You have a 60-day Special Enrollment Period. Follow the tree above within your window.
```

**Design:** Clean, color-coded arrows. Green = go this route, yellow = check eligibility, red = urgent deadline. Include healthcare.gov URL and 1-800-318-2596 phone number at bottom.

**Instructional use:** Day 1 coverage sources section. Students keep as reference card. Referenced in Scenario 2 (Marcus turns 26).

### Asset 4: The Turning-26 Timeline
**Type:** Visual timeline
**Format:** 8.5×11 horizontal, color-coded urgency zones
**File naming:** `L-72_Turning_26_Timeline.png`

**Purpose:** Show exactly what happens when you turn 26, with key actions mapped to specific time windows.

**Timeline design:**

**Day 0 (Birthday):** Parent's coverage ends
- Color: RED (critical date)
- Note: "You are now uninsured unless you enrolled ahead of time"

**Days 1-14:** Early action window
- Color: GREEN (time to act)
- Actions: Check employer coverage eligibility, check Medicaid eligibility for your state, visit healthcare.gov to compare plans

**Days 15-45:** Mid-window
- Color: YELLOW (act soon)
- Actions: Narrow down plan choices, compare total costs (premium + OOP), enroll if you haven't already

**Days 46-60:** Urgent window
- Color: ORANGE (window closing)
- Actions: ENROLL NOW. Coverage starts first day of following month.

**Day 61+:** Window closed
- Color: RED (missed deadline)
- Consequence: Uninsured until next open enrollment (November-January). No coverage for 5-10 months.

**Bottom of timeline:** "Don't wait. Most people wait until week 7 or 8 and miss the deadline. Enroll in week 1 or 2."

**Instructional use:** Day 1 Turning-26 section and Scenario 2 (Marcus). Referenced in Station 4 Scenario 1 (Sofia).

## Downloadable Resources (5 PDFs)

### Resource 1: Health Insurance Glossary
**File naming:** `L-72_Health_Insurance_Glossary.pdf`
**Format:** 2-page PDF, alphabetical list

**Contents:**
- All 10 key terms from Day 1 (premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, OOP max, network, HMO, PPO, HDHP, HSA)
- Plus 10 additional terms students will encounter in real plans:
  - **EOB (Explanation of Benefits):** Statement showing what insurance paid and what you owe
  - **Formulary:** List of prescription drugs covered by your plan
  - **Prior authorization:** Requirement to get insurer approval before certain treatments or medications
  - **Network tier:** Some plans have tiered networks (Tier 1 = lowest cost, Tier 2 = higher cost)
  - **Copay accumulator:** Some plans don't count manufacturer copay assistance toward your deductible/OOP max
  - **Grace period:** Time you have to pay a missed premium before coverage terminates (usually 30-90 days)
  - **COBRA:** Federal law allowing you to continue employer coverage after leaving job (at full cost)
  - **Summary of Benefits and Coverage (SBC):** Standardized document summarizing plan costs and coverage
  - **Qualified Life Event:** See Special Enrollment Period
  - **Metal Tier:** Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum classification based on cost-sharing levels

Each term: one-sentence definition, example usage in context.

### Resource 2: Questions to Ask HR During Open Enrollment
**File naming:** `L-72_Questions_for_HR.pdf`
**Format:** 1-page checklist

**Questions to ask:**
1. When does my health coverage start? (First day of employment? After 30/60/90 days?)
2. What plan options are available? (HMO, PPO, HDHP?)
3. How much is the employee contribution per paycheck?
4. Is there an employer contribution to HSA if I choose the HDHP?
5. What is the deductible, out-of-pocket maximum, and coinsurance for each plan option?
6. Are my current doctors in-network for any of these plans?
7. Does the plan cover prescriptions I currently take?
8. Can I add dependents (spouse, children)? How much does that cost?
9. Is there an FSA or HSA option?
10. What happens if I need to change plans mid-year? (Qualifying life events only, or can I switch anytime?)

**Bottom note:** Don't be afraid to ask these questions. HR expects them. Not asking can cost you thousands.

### Resource 3: Turning 26 Action Checklist
**File naming:** `L-72_Turning_26_Checklist.pdf`
**Format:** 1-page step-by-step checklist

**60 days before your 26th birthday:**
- [ ] Contact parent's insurance company: confirm exact date coverage ends (birthday? End of birth month?)
- [ ] Check if you have employer-sponsored health insurance (if employed full-time)
  - [ ] If yes: ask HR when you're eligible and what plans are available
  - [ ] If no: proceed to marketplace/Medicaid options
- [ ] Check Medicaid eligibility for your state: visit healthcare.gov or call 1-800-318-2596
  - [ ] Income threshold varies by state—look up your state's specific rules
- [ ] Visit healthcare.gov and compare marketplace plans
  - [ ] Enter your income to see if you qualify for subsidies
  - [ ] Compare Silver plans (subsidies maximize here)
  - [ ] Calculate total annual cost: premiums + estimated out-of-pocket

**30 days before birthday:**
- [ ] Make your decision: employer plan, Medicaid, or marketplace
- [ ] Gather required documents: Social Security number, income information, current coverage details

**Enroll by day 60:**
- [ ] Complete enrollment application
- [ ] Pay first month's premium
- [ ] Confirm coverage start date (typically first day of following month)
- [ ] Save confirmation and policy documents

**After enrollment:**
- [ ] Add new insurance card to wallet/phone
- [ ] Find in-network primary care doctor
- [ ] Set up online account with insurer
- [ ] Update emergency contacts with new coverage info

**Resources:**
- Healthcare.gov: [healthcare.gov](https://healthcare.gov)
- Phone: 1-800-318-2596
- Medicaid: Check your state's website (search "[state name] Medicaid eligibility")

### Resource 4: Healthcare.gov Walkthrough
**File naming:** `L-72_Healthcaregov_Walkthrough.pdf`
**Format:** 2-page step-by-step guide with placeholder screenshots

**Page 1: Getting Started**
1. **Visit healthcare.gov**
2. **Click "Get Coverage" or "See Plans & Prices"**
3. **Enter your ZIP code** (determines which plans are available in your area)
4. **Create an account or log in** (you'll need: email address, password, security questions)
5. **Start a new application** (or update existing if you've applied before)

**Page 2: Application & Plan Selection**
6. **Enter household information:** How many people in your household? Ages? Relationship?
7. **Enter income information:** Current annual income (or projected if unemployed/changing jobs)
8. **See if you qualify for subsidies** (system calculates automatically based on income and household size)
9. **Compare plans:**
   - Sort by monthly premium, deductible, out-of-pocket maximum
   - Filter by metal tier (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum)
   - Check if your doctors are in-network (click "See doctors & hospitals")
10. **Select a plan and enroll**
11. **Pay first month's premium** (coverage doesn't start until premium is paid)

**Important notes:**
- Screenshots are placeholders—healthcare.gov interface changes periodically
- For real-time help: call 1-800-318-2596 (free, available 24/7 in multiple languages)
- Open enrollment: usually November 1 - January 15 (coverage starts January 1)
- Special Enrollment Periods: 60 days after qualifying life event
