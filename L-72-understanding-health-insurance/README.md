# L-72: Understanding Health Insurance

## Chapter Overview

**Title:** Understanding Health Insurance
**L-Chapter ID:** 72
**Content Density:** Standard
**Template Format:** Case-study
**Day 1/Day 2 Connection:** Extension

### Purpose and Significance

Health insurance is the single area of personal finance where lack of knowledge has the most immediate and severe financial consequences. This chapter addresses the largest content gap in the standard curriculum—health insurance mechanics, navigation, and decision-making—and directly fulfills Texas §113.49(c)(12)(B) requirement to "evaluate health insurance options."

Unlike L-33 (Understanding and Managing Risk) and L-34 (Insurance as a Risk Management Tool), which cover general insurance principles, L-72 goes deep specifically on health insurance: cost-sharing structures, plan types, coverage sources, the ACA marketplace, Medicaid, HSAs, and the critical turning-26 transition.

### Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter, students will be able to:

1. Distinguish between key cost-sharing components of a health plan (premium, deductible, copay, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximum)
2. Compare plan types (HMO, PPO, EPO, HDHP) and identify the tradeoffs of each
3. Explain how employer-sponsored, ACA marketplace, Medicaid/CHIP, and Medicare coverage differ
4. Describe what happens when a young adult turns 26 and ages off a parent's plan
5. Evaluate when a high-deductible plan paired with an HSA makes financial sense

### Content Structure

**Day 1: Health Insurance Fundamentals (55 minutes)**
- Opening hook: Maya's 47-page benefits packet
- Key concepts: 10 essential health insurance terms
- Deeper exploration: Cost structure, plan types, coverage sources, turning 26, HSA decision
- Real-world examples: Jordan's first job, Marcus turns 26
- Skill Builder: Health Insurance Readiness Checklist (portfolio artifact)

**Day 2: Health Insurance Decision-Making Learning Lab (55 minutes)**
- Station 1: Health Plan Cost Calculator (calculate total annual costs under 3 plans × 3 scenarios)
- Station 2: ACA Marketplace Navigator (subsidy eligibility, metal tiers, 4 profile analyses)
- Station 3: HSA vs. FSA Decision Tool (match plan type to health status for 3 profiles)
- Station 4: Special Enrollment Periods (life events checklist, 2 scenario walk-throughs)
- Portfolio deliverable: "My Coverage Timeline" (3 life events with action plans)

### Key Differentiators

**What makes L-72 unique:**
1. **Immediate relevance:** Every student will face the turning-26 decision. This is not theoretical.
2. **Practical calculations:** Students learn to calculate total annual costs, not just premiums.
3. **SEP focus:** Special Enrollment Periods are rarely taught but critically important—missing a 60-day window can mean months of uninsured status.
4. **HSA wealth-building:** Positions HSAs as retirement accounts, not just medical spending accounts.
5. **Marketplace navigation:** Students learn the actual process (healthcare.gov, subsidies, metal tiers) not just abstract concepts.

**What L-72 does NOT duplicate:**
- L-33 covers general risk management frameworks (avoid, reduce, transfer, accept risk)
- L-34 covers insurance as a tool (premiums, deductibles, the concept of pooling risk)
- L-35 covers managing insurance costs across all insurance types
- L-72 picks up where these leave off and goes deep on health insurance specifically

### Standards Alignment

**Texas-PFL §113.49(c)(12)(B):** "The student is expected to evaluate health insurance options, including employer-provided and private insurance."
- L-72 directly addresses this standard with employer vs. marketplace vs. Medicaid comparisons, cost calculations, and plan type evaluations.

**Texas-PFLE §113.76(d)(6)(H):** "The student is expected to evaluate health insurance coverage."
- Covered through Day 2 station activities: plan comparison, subsidy analysis, HSA decision tool.

**Kentucky FL.H.24:** "Identify common types of risk management strategies, including insurance, legal contracts, emergency funds and estate planning."
- Health insurance as risk management tool (subset of broader standard)

**National Jump$tart Standard 5: Managing Risk**
- Evaluate health insurance options and coverage decisions

### Teacher Notes

**Time allocation:**
- Day 1 prioritizes foundational vocabulary and structure. Don't aim for perfect mastery—Day 2 reinforces through application.
- Day 2 can use rotation or sequential model depending on classroom setup. Prioritize Stations 1 and 4 if time is limited.

**Common student misconceptions:**
1. Deductible = monthly payment (actually, that's the premium)
2. After hitting deductible, insurance pays 100% (no—coinsurance still applies)
3. COBRA is free employer coverage continuation (no—you pay full premium)
4. Medicaid is only for unemployed people (no—working adults can qualify based on income)
5. You can enroll anytime (no—only during open enrollment or SEP windows)

**Sensitive topics:**
- Families who are currently uninsured: Respond with resources (healthcare.gov, Medicaid eligibility), not judgment
- Medical debt: Some students may have family members with medical debt—acknowledge this reality without dwelling on it

**Key teaching moment:**
The turning-26 cliff and 60-day SEP window generate the strongest emotional reactions. Use Sofia's scenario (Station 4) to make this deadline feel real and urgent.

### Implementation Notes

**For instructors new to health insurance content:**
- You don't need to be a health insurance expert to teach this chapter
- The key is directing students to the right resources (healthcare.gov, 1-800-318-2596) rather than trying to answer every state-specific question
- When a student asks about their family's plan: "Every plan is different. Check your plan's Summary of Benefits and Coverage or call the number on the insurance card."

**For districts/states with specific mandates:**
- Texas: This chapter fulfills the health insurance evaluation requirement in both PFL and PFLE standards
- Kentucky: Covers health insurance as risk management strategy (subset of FL.H.24)
- All states: Turning-26 transition and SEPs are federally consistent (ACA rules apply everywhere)

### Differentiation Support

**For struggling students:**
- Provide pre-filled glossary cards with all key terms
- Offer partially completed Station 1 grid (fill in some cells, students complete rest)
- Pair with stronger students for station work

**For ELL students:**
- Premium/copay/deductible translations if available
- Pair with fluent English speakers
- Extended time for stations

**For advanced students:**
- Extension: Visit healthcare.gov, create fictional profile, compare real plans, present recommendation
- Research question: Compare U.S. health insurance to universal healthcare in other countries
- Calculate: "At what medical bill amount does Plan A become cheaper than Plan C?"
