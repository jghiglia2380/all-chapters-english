### Overview

### Station 1: Health Plan Cost Calculator

**Purpose:** Discover that the cheapest-premium plan is not always the cheapest plan overall.

**Your Task:** You'll work with three fictional health plans and three usage scenarios. Calculate the total annual cost (premiums + out-of-pocket) for each plan under each scenario, then identify which plan wins in each case.

**The Three Plans:**

**Plan A (Gold PPO):**
- Premium: $280/month ($3,360/year)
- Deductible: $500
- Copay: $20
- Coinsurance: 10% after deductible
- Out-of-pocket maximum: $2,000

**Plan B (Silver PPO):**
- Premium: $160/month ($1,920/year)
- Deductible: $1,500
- Copay: $35
- Coinsurance: 20% after deductible
- Out-of-pocket maximum: $4,000

**Plan C (Bronze HDHP):**
- Premium: $80/month ($960/year)
- Deductible: $3,000
- No copays (pay full cost until deductible, then coinsurance)
- Coinsurance: 30% after deductible
- Out-of-pocket maximum: $6,000

**The Three Scenarios:**

**Scenario A — Healthy Year:**
- One annual physical (preventive, covered at 100%)
- One urgent care visit with X-ray ($350 total bill before insurance)

**Scenario B — Moderate Year:**
- Four primary care visits ($150 each = $600)
- One specialist visit ($250)
- One MRI ($3,500)
- One prescription refill per month ($40/month = $480)
Total bills before insurance: $4,830

**Scenario C — High-Need Year:**
- Emergency appendectomy with 2-day hospital stay ($22,000)
- Follow-up care and medications ($1,500)
Total bills before insurance: $23,500

**Calculation Grid:**

| Scenario | Plan A Total Cost | Plan B Total Cost | Plan C Total Cost | Winner |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|---------|
| Healthy Year (A) | __________ | __________ | __________ | _______ |
| Moderate Year (B) | __________ | __________ | __________ | _______ |
| High-Need Year (C) | __________ | __________ | __________ | _______ |

**Steps for each calculation:**
1. Calculate annual premium cost
2. Add out-of-pocket costs:
   - Medical bills until deductible is met (you pay 100%)
   - Medical bills after deductible (you pay coinsurance %)
   - Don't exceed the out-of-pocket maximum
3. Add them together for total annual cost

**Reflection Questions:**
1. Which plan had the cheapest premium? Did it win in any scenarios?
2. Under what circumstances would you choose Plan C over Plan A?
3. Write a 2-sentence summary: "Plan C is the cheapest if... but Plan A is the cheapest if..."

### Station 2: ACA Marketplace Navigator

**Purpose:** Learn that lower income = larger subsidies, and understand the metal tier system.

**Your Task:** For each of four fictional people, determine if they qualify for marketplace subsidies and which metal tier makes the most sense given their income and situation.

**Key Information You Need:**
- **2025 Federal Poverty Level (FPL):** $15,060 for single adult; $20,440 for 2-person household; $30,900 for family of 4
- **Subsidy eligibility:** Income between 100-400% FPL (some states extend beyond 400%)
- **Medicaid eligibility:** Varies by state; in expansion states, adults up to 138% FPL may qualify
- **Metal Tiers:**
  - **Bronze:** Lowest premium, highest cost-sharing (plan pays ~60% of costs)
  - **Silver:** Moderate premium, moderate cost-sharing (plan pays ~70%); subsidies maximize here
  - **Gold:** Higher premium, lower cost-sharing (plan pays ~80%)
  - **Platinum:** Highest premium, lowest cost-sharing (plan pays ~90%)

**The Four People:**

- Single adult, age 24
- Income: $20,000/year
- No employer coverage
- Generally healthy, sees doctor 2-3 times/year

- Recommended metal tier: __________ Why? __________

- Recommended metal tier: __________ Why? __________

**Person 3: Taylor (family of 3)**
- Two adults + one child, ages 31, 33, 4
- Combined household income: $55,000/year
- No employer coverage

*Questions:*

- Recommended metal tier: __________ Why? __________

**Person 4: Casey**
- Single adult, age 27
- Income: $65,000/year
- Employer offers affordable coverage ($180/month for employee, $450/month for family)

*Questions:*

### Station 3: HSA vs. FSA vs. Neither Decision Tool

**Purpose:** Understand when the HDHP/HSA combination makes mathematical sense.

**Your Task:** For each of three profiles, analyze their situation and recommend either HDHP+HSA, traditional PPO, or PPO+FSA. Show your math.

**Background on HSA vs. FSA:**
- **HSA (Health Savings Account):** Only available with HDHP. Triple tax advantage. Funds roll over indefinitely. 2025 contribution limit: $4,300 individual.
- **FSA (Flexible Spending Account):** Available with any non-HDHP plan. Pre-tax contributions but use-it-or-lose-it annually. 2025 limit: $3,200.

**Profile A: Riley, Age 24**
- Income: $38,000/year (22% tax bracket)
- Health status: Generally healthy, doctor visits 2x/year, no regular medications
- Savings: $2,000 emergency fund

**Employer offers:**
- HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
- PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, FSA-eligible

**Your analysis:**
1. Calculate annual premium difference: __________
2. Estimate likely out-of-pocket costs (2 visits @ $150 each): __________
3. If Riley maxes HSA contribution ($4,300), tax savings at 22%: __________
4. Total annual cost under HDHP (premiums + OOP - tax savings): __________
5. Total annual cost under PPO (premiums + OOP): __________
6. **Recommendation:** __________ **Why?** __________

**Profile B: Morgan, Age 28**
- Income: $52,000/year (22% tax bracket)
- Health status: Type 1 diabetes, sees endocrinologist quarterly
- Medications: Insulin and testing supplies ($400/month = $4,800/year)

**Employer offers:**
- HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
- PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, copays $30 for doctor/$10 for generics

**Your analysis:**
1. Under HDHP: Morgan hits $2,500 deductible after 6-7 months of insulin costs alone
2. Annual premium: HDHP = __________, PPO = __________
3. Estimated OOP under HDHP: __________ (will hit deductible, then coinsurance)
4. Estimated OOP under PPO: __________ (copays for visits + prescriptions, likely hits OOP max)
5. **Recommendation:** __________ **Why?** __________

**Profile C: Drew, Age 31**
- Income: $45,000/year (22% tax bracket)
- Health status: Pregnant, first child expected in 6 months
- Currently on PPO, open enrollment coming up

**Employer offers:**
- HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
- PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, $3,000 OOP max

**Your analysis:**
1. Pregnancy and delivery typically cost $10,000-$15,000 before insurance
2. Under HDHP: Will hit deductible immediately, then coinsurance until OOP max
3. Under PPO: Will hit $750 deductible quickly, then coinsurance until $3,000 OOP max
4. Which plan results in lower total annual cost for a high-medical-use year? __________
5. **Recommendation:** __________ **Why?** __________

**Key Takeaway:** HSAs are powerful wealth-building tools for healthy young people with low medical expenses. They're the wrong choice for people with high, predictable medical costs. Know your situation before choosing your plan.
