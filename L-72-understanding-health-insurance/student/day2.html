  • • Copay: $20
  • • Coinsurance: 10% after deductible

Plan B (Silver PPO)

  • • Premium: $160/month ($1,920/year)
  • • Deductible: $1,500

Plan C (Bronze HDHP)

  • • Premium: $80/month ($960/year)

Station 2: ACA Marketplace Navigator

20 minutes

Purpose: Learn that lower income = larger subsidies, and understand the metal tier system.

Key Information You Need:

  • • 2025 Federal Poverty Level (FPL): $15,060 for single adult; $20,440 for 2-person household; $30,900 for family of 4
  • • Subsidy eligibility: Income between 100-400% FPL (some states extend beyond 400%)
  • • Medicaid eligibility: Varies by state; in expansion states, adults up to 138% FPL may qualify
  • • Metal Tiers: Bronze (~60% coverage), Silver (~70%, subsidies maximize here), Gold (~80%), Platinum (~90%)

Person 1: Alex

Single adult, age 24 | Income: $20,000/year | No employer coverage | Generally healthy, sees doctor 2-3 times/year

Person 2: Jordan

Single adult, age 29 | Income: $35,000/year | Employer offers coverage but employee share is $420/month (14% of income) | Has asthma, needs regular prescriptions

Person 3: Taylor (family of 3)

Two adults + one child, ages 31, 33, 4 | Combined household income: $55,000/year | No employer coverage | Child has no ongoing health issues; adults generally healthy

Person 4: Casey

Single adult, age 27 | Income: $65,000/year | Employer offers affordable coverage ($180/month for employee, $450/month for family) | Currently healthy, no dependents

Important Note:

For your actual situation, visit healthcare.gov or call 1-800-318-2596. Rules vary by state and change annually. This exercise teaches you the framework—always verify current information for real decisions.

Station 3: HSA vs. FSA vs. Neither Decision Tool

20 minutes

Purpose: Understand when the HDHP/HSA combination makes mathematical sense.

Background on HSA vs. FSA:

  • • HSA (Health Savings Account): Only available with HDHP. Triple tax advantage. Funds roll over indefinitely. 2025 contribution limit: $4,300 individual.
  • • FSA (Flexible Spending Account): Available with any non-HDHP plan. Pre-tax contributions but use-it-or-lose-it annually. 2025 limit: $3,200.

Profile A: Riley, Age 24

Income: $38,000/year (22% tax bracket) | Health status: Generally healthy, doctor visits 2x/year, no regular medications | Savings: $2,000 emergency fund

Employer offers:

  • • HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
  • • PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, FSA-eligible

Profile B: Morgan, Age 28

Income: $52,000/year (22% tax bracket) | Health status: Type 1 diabetes, sees endocrinologist quarterly | Medications: Insulin and testing supplies ($400/month = $4,800/year)

Employer offers:

  • • HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
  • • PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, copays $30 for doctor/$10 for generics

Profile C: Drew, Age 31

Income: $45,000/year (22% tax bracket) | Health status: Pregnant, first child expected in 6 months | Currently on PPO, open enrollment coming up

Employer offers:

  • • HDHP: $90/month premium, $2,500 deductible, HSA-eligible
  • • PPO: $190/month premium, $750 deductible, $3,000 OOP max

Key Takeaway:

HSAs are powerful wealth-building tools for healthy young people with low medical expenses. They're the wrong choice for people with high, predictable medical costs. Know your situation before choosing your plan.
